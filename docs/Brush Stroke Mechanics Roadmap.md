# Brush Stroke Mechanics Roadmap

This is the **primary implementation roadmap for stroke realism** in Graffux.

The material-aware paint work remains useful supporting infrastructure, but the critical path is the behavior that belongs to the brush and to the act of making the stroke.

## Scope boundary

### Primary path — brushstroke mechanics

- stroke speed, acceleration and deceleration;
- pressure and contact-force evidence;
- stylus tilt and orientation evidence;
- finger contact geometry;
- rake / drag direction;
- directional bending;
- angular lag behind the hand;
- contact hysteresis / mechanical memory;
- snap-back and recovery;
- touchdown, dwell, stab, drag, flick and lift-off;
- bristle/tuft splay;
- coherent split tips;
- structured rake/comb striation generated by brush geometry;
- flat / round / filbert / rigger / fan-like morphology;
- deterministic replay of all mechanical state;
- live/commit parity for resolved brush geometry;
- bounded mobile cost.

### Secondary path — not brushstroke mechanics

These may interact with brush mechanics later but do not drive the current implementation sequence:

- pigment/color mixing;
- wet-paint transfer and reservoir chemistry;
- fluidity, viscosity and flow simulation;
- canvas/substrate surface texture or absorbency;
- wetness transport;
- diffusion/bleeding;
- impasto/material height evolution;
- optical wet/dry shading;
- spectral/Kubelka-Munk work.

Brush-tip texture caused by **bristle/tuft geometry** is in scope. Canvas surface texture is not.

## Governing architecture: physics and telemetry are co-designed

Pressure, tilt and orientation are not optional bolt-ons and they are not merely ordinary brush parameters. They are first-class observations of the way the artist is presenting and loading the brush.

At the same time, Graffux must not hard-code simplistic final mappings such as:

```text
pressure -> radius
 tilt    -> ellipse
 azimuth -> dab angle
```

That would produce a responsive stamp rather than a mechanically convincing brush.

The architecture is instead:

```text
Raw device events
        ↓
Device-specific telemetry interpretation
        ↓
values + availability + provenance + confidence
        ↓
                 ┌──────────────────────────────┐
Stroke kinematics│ pressure / tilt / orientation│ finger contact geometry
(speed, heading, │ and other expressive signals │
 acceleration)   └──────────────────────────────┘
        \                 |                 /
         \                |                /
          └────── Mechanical targets ─────┘
                         ↓
               Brush Mechanical State
          (bend, lag, compression, lean,
             hysteresis, recovery, splay)
                         ↓
                Contact / Tuft Geometry
                         ↓
                    Resolved Dabs
                         ↓
                Existing renderers
```

The telemetry contract is built **now**, alongside the physics. The exact interpretation of those signals remains replaceable and becomes more physically meaningful as the brush model gains tuft structure.

For example, today's pressure evidence may provisionally target compression/splay. Once explicit tufts exist, that same pressure evidence can control bundle compression, separation and contact engagement without changing the input pipeline or renderer contract.

## Device-aware telemetry foundation

Graffux keeps three input classes distinct instead of flattening them into one generic pointer stream.

### High-quality stylus

Expected evidence can include:

- continuous pressure;
- tilt;
- azimuth/orientation;
- high-rate historical/coalesced points;
- future additional stylus axes when useful.

These signals can receive high confidence, but their final mechanical interpretation is still mediated by stiffness, hysteresis and recovery.

### Basic stylus

A pressure-only stylus remains a **basic stylus** even when its pressure sensor is good.

- position, timing and path kinematics remain authoritative;
- pressure receives confidence based on observed usefulness/variation;
- missing tilt/orientation remain explicitly unavailable;
- absent signals are never fabricated.

### Finger

Finger input has a different physical vocabulary:

- contact major/minor dimensions;
- contact-area growth and contraction;
- touchscreen pressure when meaningful;
- touch-ellipse orientation where available;
- speed, acceleration, curvature, dwell, touchdown and lift lifecycle.

Finger contact area is not disguised as stylus tilt or azimuth. It remains touch-contact evidence so the mechanical model can use it appropriately.

### Shared intent contract

Every expressive signal carries:

- value;
- availability;
- source/provenance;
- confidence.

The brush solver consumes confidence-weighted evidence rather than assuming every Android axis is equally trustworthy.

Current implementation:

- `BrushInputTool` identifies stylus/finger/unknown input;
- `BrushTelemetryProfile` distinguishes high-quality stylus, basic stylus, finger and legacy samples;
- `BrushTelemetryMetadata` carries confidence and provenance through serialization/replay;
- `BrushTelemetryInterpreter` classifies and filters each device family independently;
- `BrushIntentTelemetry` is the renderer-independent mechanics-facing frame;
- historical samples without the new metadata remain replay-compatible through the legacy trust contract.

## Current mechanical baseline

Graffux now provides:

- canonical `BrushSample` input with device-aware telemetry metadata;
- separate high-quality stylus, basic stylus and finger telemetry interpretation;
- profile-specific pressure/speed filtering;
- pressure/tilt/orientation/contact confidence and provenance;
- deterministic `BrushSensorEngine` routes for legacy brush dynamics;
- renderer-independent `BrushContactModel`;
- persistent bend, drag angle, compression and lean state;
- speed-driven bend;
- directional lag and hysteresis;
- recovery as motion slows;
- confidence-weighted pressure/contact compression evidence;
- confidence-weighted tilt/azimuth intent evidence;
- contact-center drag;
- contact width/splay and elongation outputs;
- stateful contact angle/rake output;
- canonical `BrushStamps.dynamicDabs` mechanical integration;
- incremental `IncrementalDynamicDabGenerator` mechanical integration;
- interpolation that preserves telemetry confidence/provenance;
- speed-sensitive thinning;
- speed-adaptive start/end behavior;
- first-touch blot, dwell growth and sharpness response;
- elongated tips and per-dab angle;
- follow-stroke rotation;
- CPU/Vulkan renderers that consume already-resolved dab geometry.

Mechanics remain default-disabled for existing brushes unless their contact configuration opts in.

## Mechanical state contract

### Persistent brush state

State carried through a stroke includes or will include:

- drag/rake direction;
- bend magnitude and direction;
- angular lag;
- compression;
- lean;
- hysteresis / previous deformation;
- recovery/snap state;
- splay;
- lifecycle/contact phase;
- per-tuft displacement/separation as the next major addition.

### Renderer-independent contact output

The renderer consumes resolved geometry:

- contact center/offset;
- width/radius multiplier;
- aspect ratio;
- contact angle;
- splay;
- later: sub-tuft positions, angles, widths and contact weights.

Renderers do **not** independently reinterpret pressure, tilt, orientation or input-device class.

## Phase M1 — Stateful contact mechanics

**Status: baseline implemented; validation/hardening in progress.**

The current model resolves:

- speed-dependent bend;
- drag direction;
- stiffness-controlled lag;
- direction-change hysteresis;
- recovery as motion slows;
- pressure/contact-driven compression target;
- tilt-driven lean target;
- tilt/confidence-gated orientation steering;
- contact-center drag;
- contact width/splay;
- contact elongation;
- stateful rake angle.

Pressure/tilt/orientation already participate, but through explicit provisional couplings that can be replaced as the physical model improves.

### Exit gate

- deterministic state evolution;
- O(new dabs), never O(total stroke history);
- visible speed-dependent bend and lag;
- visible reversal hysteresis;
- confidence changes mechanical authority without changing signal provenance;
- live/canonical geometry agreement;
- disabled configuration preserves the existing static path.

---

## Phase M2 — Coarse bristle / tuft topology

**This is the next primary implementation phase.**

**Goal:** give the brush stable internal contact structure instead of representing the entire tip as one deforming ellipse.

Represent the tip as a small bounded number of named/stable sub-tufts or bristle bundles.

Each tuft should carry at least:

- stable identity;
- ferrule-local root offset;
- rest direction;
- local width/contact weight;
- stiffness;
- current bend/contact offset;
- neighbor cohesion;
- separation/split state.

The first model should be analytic/stateful, not full PBD.

### Required behavior

- pressure/compression can spread the bundle layout coherently;
- tilt/lean can change which bundles engage and how strongly;
- rake/drag direction trails bundle contacts coherently;
- speed can increase bend/separation through the same mechanical state;
- bundles keep their identities through corners and reversals;
- split tips emerge from stable neighboring bundles, never random scatter;
- stopping/lifting lets bundles recover/converge.

### Algorithm progression

1. deterministic analytic tuft layout;
2. stateful damped bundle offsets;
3. neighbor cohesion and separation thresholds;
4. constrained/PBD bundle solver only if the analytic/spring model visibly fails;
5. individual-bristle simulation only if bundle mechanics still cannot produce the required marks.

### Exit gate

- structured multi-contact marks are reproducible;
- split marks remain coherent across a stroke;
- rake/comb marks retain stable parallel identities;
- pressure/tilt/orientation can steer bundle mechanics without bypassing state;
- live/replay output agrees;
- bundle count and update cost are bounded.

## Phase M3 — Brush morphology / archetypes

**Goal:** make different brushes mechanically distinct rather than merely different static masks.

Initial archetypes:

- Round;
- Flat;
- Filbert;
- Rigger/Liner;
- Fan;
- Rake/Comb.

Examples:

- **Flat:** broad lateral bundle layout, strong presentation-angle sensitivity, edge/broad-side transitions.
- **Round:** radial bundle distribution, symmetric rest shape, pressure-driven radial splay.
- **Filbert:** rounded flat arrangement with softer edge transitions.
- **Rigger:** long lag, narrow contact, strong trailing behavior.
- **Fan:** wide bundle spread whose engagement changes with lean/presentation.
- **Rake/Comb:** stable parallel bundle contacts with coherent directional striation.

The same telemetry frame drives every archetype, but morphology changes what that evidence means physically.

---

## Phase M4 — Touchdown, dwell, cornering and lift-off

**Goal:** make contact transitions part of the same stateful tuft system.

Graffux already has useful taper/blot behavior. Consolidate and extend it rather than discarding it.

Focus on:

- first-contact compression;
- initial tuft engagement/spread;
- dwell growth of deformation;
- transition from stab to drag;
- settled drag;
- cornering;
- reversal snap;
- slowing/stopping recovery;
- fast flick exit;
- slow deliberate lift-off;
- tuft convergence during lift.

Pressure/contact evidence helps identify loading/unloading, but lifecycle inference must also work for basic styluses and fingers using kinematics/contact evidence.

---

## Phase M5 — Intent-model refinement

This is **not** when telemetry is first introduced. Telemetry is already in the architecture.

M5 means replacing provisional couplings with better empirically informed mappings after tuft mechanics provides meaningful physical targets.

Examples:

- pressure → bundle compression, engagement depth, splay and split threshold;
- tilt → asymmetric bundle engagement, side-vs-point contact and bend bias;
- orientation → ferrule presentation and archetype-specific rake target;
- finger major/minor/orientation → contact footprint intent without pretending the finger is a stylus;
- motion/telemetry disagreement → confidence-weighted target resolved through stiffness/hysteresis rather than an instantaneous angle jump.

The telemetry API and serialized sample format should not need redesign for this refinement.

---

## Phase M6 — Native/GPU and performance hardening

Most mechanics should remain renderer-independent and resolve before drawing.

Required gates:

- CPU/Vulkan consume identical resolved geometry;
- no renderer-specific reinterpretation of pressure, tilt, orientation, contact shape or speed;
- incremental mechanics cost stays bounded with stroke length;
- no unbounded tuft solver queue;
- capability tiers can reduce tuft count or solver complexity;
- the basic/static brush remains the latency baseline.

---

## Priority statement

When choosing between a material improvement and a brushstroke-mechanics improvement, **brushstroke mechanics wins** unless material work is required to preserve correctness.

Within brushstroke mechanics, physics and telemetry are developed together: **telemetry is first-class now, while its interpretation becomes progressively more physically meaningful as the brush model gains real tuft structure.**
